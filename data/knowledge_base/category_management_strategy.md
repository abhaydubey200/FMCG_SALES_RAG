# Category Management Strategy

## Section 1: Category Roles

Each category is assigned a strategic role at the account level:
Destination (drives store choice — typically Beverages and Packaged
Foods staples), Routine (steady repeat-purchase volume — most Home Care
and Personal Care), Occasional/Seasonal (Snacks around key occasions),
and Convenience (impulse-driven, smaller pack sizes).

## Section 2: Shelf Share Principles

Shelf share (linear shelf space allocated to a brand within a category)
should track closely with the brand's share of category sales at that
account — a brand materially over- or under-indexed on shelf share
relative to sales share is a signal worth investigating: over-indexed
may mean the category captain relationship is working well and should be
protected, while under-indexed may indicate a listing or distribution
gap worth addressing with the account.

## Section 3: Category Captain Role

Where we hold Category Captain status with an account (typically Modern
Trade accounts where we hold the largest category share), we provide
planogram recommendations for the full category, not just our own
products — planograms that are seen to unfairly favor the captain's own
brands damage the captain relationship and risk losing the status at the
next category review.

## Section 4: New Product Introduction (NPI) Space Allocation

New product listings require freeing shelf space, typically by
delisting the lowest-velocity SKU within the same subcategory rather than
proportionally shrinking every existing SKU's facing count — proportional
shrinkage tends to reduce visibility below the threshold where even
otherwise-healthy SKUs lose sales, while delisting one genuinely
low-velocity SKU has a smaller net category impact.

## Section 5: Category Review Cadence

Full category reviews (assortment, planogram, pricing architecture)
happen twice yearly with Modern Trade accounts and annually with major
distributors for General Trade. Between formal reviews, velocity data
should still be monitored monthly, since a SKU trending toward
low-velocity status is easier to address with in-store support before the
next formal review than to defend as a delisting candidate at review time.
