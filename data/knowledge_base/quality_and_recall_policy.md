Quality and Recall Policy

Section 1: Batch Tracking Requirement

Every production batch is assigned a unique batch code printed on
consumer packaging, linked internally to: production date, production
line, raw material lot numbers, and initial distribution destinations.
Batch traceability must resolve from a single consumer-facing package
back to its full production and distribution record within 4 hours of a
quality inquiry being raised.

Section 2: Complaint Threshold for Escalation

A quality investigation is automatically opened when either of the
following is met within a rolling 30-day window for a single SKU: 5 or
more consumer complaints citing the same defect type, or a complaint rate
exceeding 0.1% of units sold in that window, whichever threshold is
reached first.

Section 3: Recall Classification

Recalls are classified by severity: Class I (reasonable probability of
serious health consequences — immediate recall, regulatory notification
within 24 hours), Class II (temporary or reversible health consequence —
recall within 72 hours), and Class III (unlikely to cause adverse health
consequence, e.g. mislabeling without a safety issue — market withdrawal
at next standard distribution cycle, no emergency timeline required).

Section 4: Shelf Life and Rotation

Distributors and direct accounts are contractually required to follow
first-expiry-first-out (FEFO) stock rotation. Products within 25% of
their shelf life remaining at the point of delivery should be flagged for
priority sell-through support (secondary display, minor promotional
support) rather than allowed to age toward the expiry date on the shelf,
since expired-stock write-offs are both a direct cost and a source of
retailer relationship friction.

Section 5: Relationship to Marketing and Trade Spend

No trade promotion or marketing campaign may be approved for a SKU
currently under an open quality investigation (see Section 2), regardless
of the promotion's projected volume or ROI — driving additional demand
toward a product under active quality investigation risks both consumer
harm and disproportionate brand-reputation damage if the investigation
confirms a defect after promoted volume has already reached consumers.
