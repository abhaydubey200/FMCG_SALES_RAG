# Brand Marketing Playbook

## Section 1: ATL and BTL Investment Split

Marketing investment is split between Above-The-Line (ATL — mass media,
brand awareness, not directly tied to a single transaction) and
Below-The-Line (BTL — trade promotions, sampling, in-store activation,
directly tied to purchase). Destination and Routine category roles
(Section 1 of Category Management Strategy) generally warrant a higher
ATL share to build durable brand equity, while Occasional/Seasonal and
Convenience roles lean BTL-heavy to convert impulse and occasion-driven
demand.

## Section 2: Brand Equity Tracking

Brand equity is tracked quarterly via: unaided awareness, purchase
intent, and price premium tolerance (the percentage price premium
consumers say they'd accept versus the category's lowest-priced
competitor). A brand with strong equity but declining recent sales should
be investigated for distribution/availability gaps before assuming the
brand itself is underperforming — strong equity with poor availability is
a different problem than weak equity, and requires a different fix
(distribution, not creative).

## Section 3: New Product Development (NPD) Gate Process

New product concepts pass through four gates: Concept (consumer research
validates the need), Formulation (product meets cost/quality targets),
Market Test (limited regional launch with defined success metrics), and
National Rollout. A concept that fails to hit its Market Test volume
target within the first two quarters should not automatically proceed to
National Rollout without a documented root-cause review — the most common
root causes are distribution gaps (not listed widely enough to reach the
target), not demand gaps (the concept itself), and these require very
different remediation.

## Section 4: Sampling Program Guidelines

Sampling programs are most effective for genuinely new-to-consumer
products or significant reformulations, not for routine volume-driving
of established SKUs — sampling an already-trial ed, mature product mostly
reaches existing buyers rather than genuinely new triers, which makes it
an inefficient use of sampling budget compared to a trade promotion for
that use case.

## Section 5: Digital and Social Investment

Digital/social investment is weighted toward categories with higher
purchase-consideration research behavior (Personal Care, select Packaged
Foods) and lower for categories that are largely habitual, low
-consideration repeat purchases (most Home Care), where in-store and
trade mechanics tend to outperform digital awareness spend on a
cost-per-incremental-unit basis.
